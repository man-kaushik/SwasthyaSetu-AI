# SwasthyaSetu AI Demo Video Script

**Target runtime:** 3-5 minutes (approximately 4 minutes at a steady pace)  
**Demo account:** Operations Manager  
**Core story:** Detect risk -> understand it with Google AI -> propose a transfer -> approve and track the response

## Recording Setup

- Use the seeded demo records, not real patient or personal data. The sample includes PHCs in Maharashtra, Bihar, and Tamil Nadu.
- Sign in with an Operations Manager account. Confirm the dashboard and Google Maps load.
- For the AI segment, deploy the protected API with a working `GEMINI_API_KEY`; verify an alert explanation is returned by Gemini before recording. Do not present a fallback response as a live Gemini result.
- For the end-to-end transfer, use the seeded ORS shortage at **MH-PUNE-PHC-003** and confirm the recommendation identifies a surplus source. Make sure Firestore reads and writes are available.
- Have the browser at **Overview** and reset Emergency mode to **Normal** before starting.

## Timed Walkthrough

### 0:00-0:35 | Problem and solution

**On screen:** Start on **Overview** with the inventory summary visible.

**Say:**

> Across India, a medicine shortage at one Primary Health Centre can be difficult to spot until patients are already affected. SwasthyaSetu AI brings facility stock, demand, and resource signals into one operations view, so teams can identify risk earlier and coordinate a response. This is a working prototype using realistic, synthetic multi-state data.

### 0:35-1:15 | Find the risk

**On screen:** Point to the critical and warning counts. Filter the inventory to Maharashtra and search for **ORS** at **MH-PUNE-PHC-003**. Show stock, daily use, days remaining, and risk.

**Say:**

> Here I’m narrowing the network to a facility and medicine that need attention. The dashboard shows current stock alongside daily consumption and estimated days remaining. In this hosted demo path, that risk signal is calculated from stock and consumption. The full-cloud data pipeline also includes BigQuery ML ARIMA_PLUS, trained over PHC-and-medicine demand histories to produce a 14-day forecast; that model output is a separate pipeline from this no-billing dashboard view.

### 1:15-1:45 | Explore an emergency scenario

**On screen:** Select **Simulate Dengue Surge**. Pause on the demand impact and updated risks, then return to **Normal**.

**Say:**

> During an outbreak, yesterday’s demand may not be a good guide to tomorrow. I’ll use the dengue what-if control to increase demand assumptions for relevant medicines and see how the displayed risk changes. This is a local demo simulation, not a live outbreak feed, and it does not overwrite the source inventory.

### 1:45-2:35 | Ask Google AI

**On screen:** Open **Alerts**, choose Hindi in the language selector, and activate **Why this alert?** for the ORS shortage. Show the returned explanation.

**Say:**

> Now I’ll ask Google Gemini to explain this alert in Hindi. The protected API sends the facility, medicine, stock, and risk context to Gemini and returns an explanation with a recommended action and a local-language message. This makes the alert easier to act on across language communities, while keeping the API key on the server. The model supports decision-making; a health official still reviews the recommendation.

### 2:35-3:35 | Turn the alert into action

**On screen:** Return to **Overview**, find the same critical ORS row, and select **Generate Transfer Plan**. Show the suggested source, destination, quantity, and distance. Open **Transfers**, approve the proposed transfer, and show its **In transit** status. If time permits, mark it completed.

**Say:**

> The next step is a practical response. The prototype checks for a nearby facility with available surplus and creates a proposed transfer for review. I can see where the medicine is moving, approve the plan, and follow its status from proposal to transit and delivery. In this demo, approval is an operator action; the system assists coordination rather than dispatching medicine autonomously.

### 3:35-4:10 | Show reach and close

**On screen:** Open **National Map** and show facilities across the sample states. Apply a risk or medicine filter.

**Say:**

> The map and state and district filters show how the same workflow can extend beyond one city. Today’s records are a sample, not national coverage. A pilot could onboard state facility and inventory feeds, validate forecasts with local teams, and expand district by district. SwasthyaSetu AI demonstrates the loop from visibility, to early warning, to a human-approved redistribution response.

## Recording Checklist

- Narrate only figures visible in the demo; don't invent impact totals or claim national deployment.
- Confirm the Hindi explanation is generated by Gemini, not the API fallback. If Gemini is unavailable, fix the connection or clearly disclose that the AI segment is not live.
- The emergency control is a what-if simulation. The lightweight dashboard calculates risk from stock and consumption; do not describe that screen as displaying the BigQuery ML forecast.
- The seeded dataset is synthetic and spans selected states. Describe it as a scale-ready prototype, not a deployed national network.
- Keep the transfer approval visible as a human decision point. User actions vary by role.
